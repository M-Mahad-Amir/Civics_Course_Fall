# MohallyWala

**Your mohalla. Your voice.**
A neighbourhood platform where residents report local problems, track them to a confirmed fix, and organise with their community. This repository is a clickable front-end prototype built for a Civics and Community Engagement course.

---

## Run it locally

No installation, server or internet connection needed.

1. Download the three files into one folder:
   - `index.html`
   - `styles.css`
   - `app.js`
2. Double-click `index.html`. It opens in your browser (Chrome, Edge or Firefox).

That's it. Your actions (reports, supports, votes) are saved in your browser's local storage. To start fresh, use the **Reset demo** option in the app.

> **Note:** This is a prototype. All data is hard-coded demo data with fictional names, and some buttons are placeholders. There is no backend, login or database.

## Why this project

Our survey of 56 residents found that people are willing to take part, but the system doesn't work for them:

- **76.8%** feel their voice has little or no impact on local officials.
- **67.9%** have never attended a community meeting in person.
- **67.9%** would use a mobile app to report problems directly.
- **87.5%** are open to joining or promoting a local community group.
- The top concerns are roads (64.3%), safety (60.7%), water (51.8%) and sanitation (50.0%).

The problem isn't a lack of willingness. It's a lack of trust, follow-through and local organisation. MohallyWala is designed around that gap.

*Caveat: the sample is small and young (74.5% aged 18–25), so we treat these results as strong signals to test further, not as facts about the whole city.*

## What the prototype shows

- **Choose your mohalla.** Each registered mohalla has its own space, set up with its local committee and groups.
- **Report and track.** Report an issue (optionally anonymously), see a status timeline, and get notified when it moves.
- **Support and confirm.** Neighbours back an issue with "Me too", and residents confirm a fix before it closes, or reopen it.
- **Authority view.** A simple inbox for the responsible office to assign and update issues.
- **Accountability board.** How responsive each department has been.
- **Community.** Groups, a committee page, meetings with RSVP, and polls.
- **English / Urdu** with right-to-left layout.

## What already exists, and where we fit

Civic reporting tools already exist in Karachi, including Karachiwala, the Pakistan Citizen's Portal and utility-company apps. We see them as proof the need is real, not as competition.

> "Karachiwala is a good example of what exists, and it shows the need is real. But a public map of problems doesn't solve what our survey found: 76.8% still feel unheard, and 67.9% have never attended a community meeting. Reporting is largely solved; organising isn't. MohallyWala is the neighbourhood layer on top. It connects the report to a local committee, a meeting, a decision and a confirmed fix. It could even feed city-wide platforms like Karachiwala rather than compete with them."

| Existing tools focus on | MohallyWala adds |
|---|---|
| Reporting and a city-wide map | One mohalla at a time, run with its local committee |
| Documenting problems publicly | Turning reports into collective action |
| Status changes | Resident confirmation before an issue closes |
| Individual complaints | Meetings, polls and groups to organise neighbours |

## Prototype limits

| Real in the prototype | Simulated |
|---|---|
| Screens, navigation and the report flow | Users and login |
| Status timeline and accountability numbers | Authority responses (triggered by a demo button) |
| Language switch, polls, RSVP | Notifications and shared data across devices |
